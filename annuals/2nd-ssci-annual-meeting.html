Call for Abstracts

Second SSCI Annual Combustion Meeting

The Singapore Section of the Combustion Institute (SSCI) is delighted to announce the 2nd SSCI Annual Combustion Meeting, to be held on 16 November 2026 at Level 2 Theatrette, CREATE Tower, 1 Create Way, Singapore, 138602.

Following the highly successful inaugural meeting, the second annual meeting will serve as a forum for researchers, practitioners, and students from academia, industry, and government to present recent advances in combustion research, exchange ideas, and establish new collaborations. Under the theme “Future Combustion: Science, Innovation and Sustainability”, the program will feature technical oral presentations together with a poster session showcasing emerging research and innovations.

Topics of Interest

We invite the submission of abstracts for consideration for oral or poster presentations in all areas of combustion science, including but not limited to:

  • Fundamental Combustion Science — chemical kinetics, flame dynamics, turbulent combustion, combustion instabilities, diagnostics
  • Advanced Combustion Technologies — engines, gas turbines, industrial combustion, propulsion systems, novel combustion concepts
  • Sustainable and Low-Carbon Combustion — hydrogen, ammonia, sustainable aviation fuels, bio-fuels, e-fuels, carbon-neutral combustion
  • Combustion Modelling, Simulation, and Control — CFD, reacting flow modelling, combustion control, AI/data-driven methods, digital twins
  • Emissions, Fire, and Energy Systems — pollutant formation and mitigation, fire safety, battery fires, carbon capture, integrated energy systems

Keynote Speakers

Prof. Yiguang Ju

Princeton University

Challenges and opportunities of ammonia combustion for power generation

Ammonia is a potential hydrogen carrier for power generation because of its higher energy density and easier storage and transportation. However, ammonia slips, high NOx/N2O emissions, and fast metal surface nitridation have made it challenging in direct applications to existing gas turbine and diesel engine applications.

In this lecture, we will first discuss the potential impact of ammonia combustion on emissions, climate, and metal surface nitridation. Then, we will present the results of NOx/N2O of high pressure (100 atm) low temperature oxidation. The three important low temperature ammonia oxidation kinetic pathways and its impact on warm flames and detonation transition will be presented.

After that, direct measurements of critical radicals such as NHx/HO2 in ammonia combustion using Faraday rotational spectroscopy and femtosecond laser absorption will be discussed. The data will be used to determine the rates of important elementary reactions such as HO2+NH2 and examine the metal surface nitridation mechanism.

Finally, a new concept of high pressure fully premixed turbulent jet stirred ammonia combustion will be presented. The potential of plasma assisted ammonia combustion for low NOx emissions will also be discussed.

Yiguang Ju is the Robert Porter Patterson Professor in Mechanical and Aerospace Engineering, Associated Faculty of Princeton Plasma Physics Laboratory, and is the Director of DOE Energy Earthshot Research Center (EERC) for plasma assisted hydrogen production.

Ju’s research interests include combustion, green fuels, plasma, and electrified manufacturing. Ju is a Fellow of The American Institute of Aeronautics and Astronautics (AIAA), The American Society of Mechanical Engineers (ASME), and The Combustion Institute.

He has received many awards such as the Friedrich Wilhelm Bessel Research Award from the Alexander von Humboldt Foundation, the AIAA propellant and combustion award, the Alfred C. Egerton Gold Medal from the Combustion Institute, the 2025 and 2026 R&D 100 Awards by R&D World magazine, and the international award from Tohoku University (2026).
